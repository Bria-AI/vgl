2. Motion blur, the signature of the style. A slow shutter and a moving camera blur the image heavily, and the blur falls on the person, not only on the background: hair, the edges of the face, hands and clothes are smeared into streaks in the direction of the movement. 3. Trails. Movement during the exposure can leave trails: the face or body smeared into a semi-transparent streak beside itself, blended with it like a double exposure. A trail is always part of the same person, never a second, separate figure.
4. Light. Soft, plain light: daylight, a window or a single soft source, sometimes the burst of an on-camera flash during a slow exposure, which leaves part of the person clear and the rest smeared. Shadows are soft.
5. Faded color. The colors are those of an aged 1960s print: low saturation, low contrast, lifted blacks, whites turned cream, and the color shift of old film, a warm yellow or magenta-pink cast in the light with faded cyan-green in the shadows and reds drifting toward orange-pink. A single color accent may stand out, still faded. Warm light is pale and soft, never orange or saturated gold.
6. Mood. Romantic, intimate, surreal and slightly otherworldly: atmosphere and emotion come before detail.
7. Pose. Expressions are inward (eyes closed, gaze turned away, lips softly parted) and gestures are soft (hands near the face, hair or fabric moving); foreground elements such as flowers or fabric may veil part of the face.
